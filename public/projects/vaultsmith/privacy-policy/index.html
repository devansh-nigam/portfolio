Privacy Policy

Effective 4 October 2026

In short

Vaultsmith keeps your cards encrypted on your own phone. The app has no internet access, no account, no analytics and no ads. We collect nothing, and nothing you store in it ever reaches us.

This policy explains what Vaultsmith, the Android app (com.vaultsmith.app) made by Devansh Nigam, does with information. "We" means the developer; "you" means the person using the app.

What the app stores, and where

Everything below is stored only on your phone. None of it is sent to us or to anyone else.

  • Your cards – card number, expiry date, CVV, cardholder name, and the label, bank, card type and colour you choose. They are encrypted with AES-256-GCM, under a key protected by your phone's secure hardware (Android Keystore), so a copy of the file is unreadable on any other device.
  • Your way back in – if you set a master password and security questions, neither is stored as written. They are turned into encryption keys on the phone (Argon2id), used only to unlock your vault.
  • Fingerprint, face and screen lock – if you use them, Android checks them. The app never receives your fingerprint, face data or PIN; it is only told whether the check succeeded.
  • Settings – your theme, colours, logo, card order and similar choices, kept in ordinary app storage so the app can look right before it is unlocked. They contain no card details.

What we collect

Nothing. Vaultsmith does not request internet access from Android, so it cannot send data anywhere. There are no accounts, servers, analytics, advertising, tracking or crash-reporting services in the app.

When information leaves your phone

Only when you choose, and only through Android:

  • Sharing a card. "Share card details" opens Android's share sheet and hands the app you pick an image and text with the card number, cardholder name and expiry date. The CVV is never shared. What happens next is up to the app you shared with.
  • Copying. A copied card number is marked as sensitive, so Android hides it in clipboard previews, and Vaultsmith clears it from the clipboard after a minute if it is still there.
  • Writing to us. "Report an issue" opens your own email app with our address and, at the end of the message, the app version and your Android version. We receive only what you choose to send, and use it only to reply. Nothing from your vault is attached.

Purchases

Vaultsmith Pro is a one-time purchase made through Google Play. Google handles the payment under the Google Privacy Policy; we never see your payment details. The app receives only Google's signed confirmation that Pro is owned, which it checks and keeps on your phone so Pro works offline. Google Play may restore the purchase on a new phone signed in to the same Google account.

Backups, and losing your phone

Vaultsmith turns off Android's cloud backup for its data, so your vault is never copied to Google Drive or anywhere else. The other side of that: if you lose your phone, or uninstall the app, your vault cannot be recovered by anyone, including us.

Screenshots

The app blocks screenshots and screen recording, so card details cannot be captured from the screen.

Permissions

  • Biometric and fingerprint – to unlock the vault with your fingerprint or face, if you choose to.
  • Vibration – for small haptic feedback.
  • Google Play Billing – to sell and verify Vaultsmith Pro.

The app does not ask for internet, location, contacts, camera, microphone or storage access.

Deleting your data

Your data exists only on your phone. Use Erase in the app to delete your vault, or uninstall Vaultsmith to remove everything it stored. Because we hold no copy, there is nothing for us to delete on our side.

Children

Vaultsmith is meant for adults and is not directed at children.

Security

We designed Vaultsmith so that your data stays encrypted and on your device. No system is perfectly secure: choose a strong master password, keep your phone's screen lock on, and keep your phone updated.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date. If the app ever started collecting data, this page would say so before that version is released.
